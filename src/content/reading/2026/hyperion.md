_Hyperion_ took its time pulling me in. Described to me as "_Canterbury Tales_ in space",
the book is composed of vignettes told by each character interspersed with present-day
interludes after they've all found themselves heading toward the planet Hyperion.

The prologue was pretty jarring from a worldbuilding perspective. A swamp on an alien
planet, a personal spaceship equipped with a Steinway piano and a wet bar stocked with
Scotch. Most faster-than-light space operas I've read, like _Dune_ or _Foundation_, have
wholly lost the memory of Earth and certainly lost actual artifacts from the 20th century.

After that opening I was skeptical that I would enjoy the stories set in this
universe, but the vignette structure worked really well. Each revealed a new slice of the
society these characters live in, and by the midpoint it all started to fit together. The
Hegemony of _Hyperion_ has a few technologies beyond our understanding of physics, but
that technology comes with real constraints and _certainly_ comes with real costs and
consequences. In between reading sessions I found myself thinking more about how such a
society would function. Taking up headspace when I'm not reading is one of my hallmarks of
a good book, and _Hyperion_ cleared that bar.

Given all of that I don't feel like _Hyperion_ stuck the landing. The last vignette, which
the book kept building up throughout, revealed twists along with large exposition dumps
that didn't feel properly set up by the preceding chapters. I think I like where Simmons
was moving towards in the end, but he put too much emphasis on unexpected twists rather
than building his authorial point of view through the perspectives of all his characters.

The present day narrative itself ended in anticlimax to be continued in the sequel, _The
Fall of Hyperion_. Unsatisfying endings normally either rush to an unearned climax or fail
to reach one at all. Simmons somehow did both in the final chapters of _Hyperion_.

I ultimately feel thoroughly invested in the universe of the book but disappointed in the
narrative, which isn't where I thought I'd be after reading the first chapters.
